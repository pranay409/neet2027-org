Chemical Coordination for NEET: Endocrine Glands and Hormones Explained

📅 October 06, 2026 📚 Biology
NEET Biology

Chemical coordination is one of the most important topics in NEET Biology, accounting for 5-8 marks in the exam. The endocrine system, comprised of specialized glands that secrete hormones, plays a crucial role in maintaining homeostasis and regulating vital body functions. This comprehensive guide covers the endocrine glands, hormones, their mechanisms, and exam-specific patterns you need to master.

Understanding the Endocrine System and Its Role (NCERT Class 11, Chapter 22)

The endocrine system is a collection of glands that produce and release hormones directly into the bloodstream. Unlike the nervous system's rapid response, hormonal responses are slower but long-lasting, making them ideal for regulating metabolic processes, growth, reproduction, and stress responses.

Key Characteristics of Hormones:

  • Secreted by endocrine glands into the bloodstream for distribution
  • Target cell specific – only cells with appropriate receptors respond
  • Low concentration effectiveness – even minute amounts produce significant effects
  • Long-lasting effects compared to neurotransmitters
  • Destroyed by enzymes after producing effects to prevent prolonged action

The NEET exam frequently tests your understanding of hormone classification (protein, steroid, prostaglandins), the difference between endocrine and exocrine glands, and the mechanism of hormone action. You must know that hormones work through two main pathways: second messenger system (cAMP pathway for peptide hormones) and direct gene activation (steroid hormones entering the nucleus).

🎯 NEET Exam Pattern Note: Expect 2-3 MCQs on hormone mechanisms, classification, and endocrine vs. exocrine glands. Questions often ask to identify hormones from descriptions of their functions.

Major Endocrine Glands and Their Hormones (NCERT Class 11, Chapter 22)

The Pituitary Gland (Master Gland)

The pituitary gland is attached to the hypothalamus and divided into anterior and posterior portions, each secreting distinct hormones. This is a high-yield topic for NEET, with 2-3 questions commonly asked.

Gland Division Hormones Secreted Functions
Anterior Pituitary Growth Hormone (GH), TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, Prolactin Growth, thyroid stimulation, stress response, reproductive function, lactation
Posterior Pituitary ADH (Vasopressin), Oxytocin Water reabsorption, uterine contractions, milk ejection

Thyroid Gland

The thyroid secretes T3 (Triiodothyronine), T4 (Thyroxine), and Calcitonin. T3 and T4 regulate metabolic rate, growth, and development. Calcitonin lowers blood calcium levels. Thyroid disorders (hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism, goiter) are frequently tested in NEET exams through case-based questions.

Parathyroid Gland

Secretes Parathyroid Hormone (PTH), which increases blood calcium levels by stimulating bone resorption and calcium reabsorption in kidneys. The antagonistic relationship between PTH and Calcitonin is essential for exam preparation.

Pancreas (Islets of Langerhans)

The pancreas contains alpha cells (secreting glucagon) and beta cells (secreting insulin). These hormones maintain blood glucose homeostasis. Diabetes mellitus (Type 1 and Type 2) questions appear frequently in NEET Biology sections. You must understand the differences between insulin-dependent and non-insulin-dependent diabetes.

Adrenal Glands

The adrenal cortex secretes glucocorticoids (cortisol), mineralocorticoids (aldosterone), and androgens. The adrenal medulla secretes epinephrine and norepinephrine during stress, triggering the "fight or flight" response. Addison's disease and Cushing's syndrome are commonly referenced in NEET questions.

Reproductive Glands

Testes secrete testosterone (development of male secondary characteristics), and ovaries secrete estrogen and progesterone (female secondary characteristics and menstrual regulation). The hormonal control of reproduction is extensively covered in NCERT Class 12 Chapter 3 and appears in 2-3 NEET questions.

Hormone Mechanisms and Regulation (NCERT Class 11, Chapter 22)

Peptide Hormones vs. Steroid Hormones

Peptide hormones (insulin, glucagon, adrenaline) cannot cross cell membranes. They bind to receptors on the cell surface, triggering the cAMP (second messenger) pathway inside the cell. Steroid hormones (testosterone, estrogen, cortisol) are lipid-soluble and cross cell membranes directly, activating gene transcription in the nucleus.

Negative Feedback Mechanism

Most hormone secretion is controlled by negative feedback loops. When hormone levels rise, they inhibit further secretion. For example, high blood glucose inhibits glucagon secretion, and high cortisol inhibits ACTH release. Understanding this mechanism is crucial for answering NEET questions about hormone regulation.

Hypothalamic-Pituitary Axis

The hypothalamus produces releasing hormones that stimulate anterior pituitary hormone secretion. This axis controls the thyroid, adrenal, and reproductive systems. Questions testing the HPG axis (Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Gonadal) and HPA axis (Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal) are common in NEET exams.

Essential Hormone Functions to Memorize for NEET:

  • Insulin: Lowers blood glucose; promotes glucose uptake and storage
  • Glucagon: Raises blood glucose; promotes glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis
  • Thyroxine (T4): Increases metabolic rate; essential for growth and development
  • Parathyroid Hormone: Increases blood calcium; antagonistic to calcitonin
  • Epinephrine: Fight-or-flight response; increases heart rate and blood pressure
  • Cortisol: Stress hormone; increases blood glucose and suppresses immunity
  • Testosterone: Male secondary sexual characteristics; spermatogenesis
  • Estrogen & Progesterone: Female secondary characteristics; menstrual regulation; pregnancy maintenance
  • ADH (Vasopressin): Increases water reabsorption in kidneys; controls osmotic pressure
  • Oxytocin: Uterine contractions during labor; milk ejection during lactation
