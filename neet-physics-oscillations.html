Oscillations for NEET: SHM Concepts That Show Up Every Year

Physics | Published: October 16, 2026 | NEET2027.org

Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) and oscillations appear in nearly every NEET exam with 3-5 direct questions and additional applications in thermodynamics, waves, and circuits. Understanding the recurring concepts and problem types is essential for scoring consistently in Physics. This guide covers the exact topics that appear year after year.

1. Core SHM Definitions and Equations (NCERT Class 11, Chapter 14)

The foundation of oscillations rests on recognizing when motion qualifies as SHM and applying the correct mathematical framework. NEET examiners test this through both conceptual questions and numerical problems.

SHM Condition and Standard Equation

Motion is classified as SHM when the restoring force is directly proportional to displacement and acts opposite to it. This fundamental principle appears in every exam under different disguises.

Restoring Force: F = -kx (where k is spring constant or equivalent) Acceleration: a = -ω²x Displacement: x(t) = A sin(ωt + φ) Velocity: v(t) = Aω cos(ωt + φ) Acceleration: a(t) = -Aω² sin(ωt + φ) Period: T = 2π/ω or T = 2π√(m/k)

Exam Pattern Alert: NEET asks "which of the following exhibits SHM?" with systems like vertical springs, simple pendulums at small angles, and LC circuits. You must quickly identify the restoring force mechanism.

Phase and Phase Difference

Phase (φ) determines the initial state of oscillation. NEET questions frequently compare phase differences between velocity and displacement, or between two oscillating bodies. Key relationship: velocity leads displacement by 90°, and acceleration leads displacement by 180°.

💡 NEET Tip: In SHM, when displacement is maximum, velocity is zero, and acceleration is maximum. When displacement is zero, velocity is maximum, and acceleration is zero. These relationships appear in 40% of SHM questions—commit them to memory.

2. Energy Conservation in Oscillatory Motion (NCERT Class 11, Chapter 14)

Energy analysis is the second most tested concept after basic equations. Examiners present problems where you must convert between kinetic and potential energy, identify total mechanical energy, or analyze damping.

Mechanical Energy in SHM

Total Energy: E = (1/2)kA² = (1/2)mω²A² Kinetic Energy: KE = (1/2)mv² = (1/2)kA²sin²(ωt + φ) Potential Energy: PE = (1/2)kx² = (1/2)kA²cos²(ωt + φ) At any point: E = KE + PE = constant (no damping)

Recurring Question Type: "At what fraction of amplitude is KE equal to PE?" or "What is velocity when particle is at displacement x?" These require you to use energy conservation equations directly.

Graphical Representation

NEET frequently shows energy vs. time or energy vs. displacement graphs. You must recognize that PE follows a cos² curve while KE follows a sin² curve, both oscillating at twice the frequency of displacement.

Exam Pattern (Last 5 Years): 2-3 questions on energy appear each year, often combined with other topics like spring systems or pendulums. One question typically involves finding velocity at a specific position using energy conservation.

3. Pendulums and Coupled Systems (NCERT Class 11, Chapter 14)

Simple pendulum problems dominate NEET applicants' weak points because they require understanding the small-angle approximation and various affecting factors.

Simple Pendulum Characteristics

Time Period: T = 2π√(L/g) Angular Frequency: ω = √(g/L) Effective Length: L_eff includes pivot adjustments Maximum velocity: v_max = √(2gL(1 - cosθ₀)) For small angles: v_max ≈ Aω where A ≈ Lθ₀

Critical Concept: Time period of simple pendulum depends ONLY on length and gravity—NOT on mass or amplitude (for small angles). NEET tests this through scenarios asking "what happens if pendulum material changes?" or "if moved to different altitude?"

Physical Pendulum and Compound Pendulum

Less common but appearing occasionally: a rigid body oscillating about a fixed axis. The moment of inertia and distance of center of mass from pivot determine the period. These questions often combine rotational dynamics with oscillation concepts.

🎯 High-Yield Topic: Questions asking "at what length does a simple pendulum have period T?" or "two pendulums have what length ratio for period ratio 1:2?" appear almost every alternate year. Practice numerical variations thoroughly.

Coupled Oscillators (Brief)

While detailed coupled oscillator analysis exceeds NEET scope, questions sometimes present two masses connected by springs. Understanding that they can oscillate in phase (symmetric) or out of phase (antisymmetric) with different frequencies helps solve these rare but high-value questions.

4. Damped Oscillations and Resonance (NCERT Class 11, Chapter 14)

This final section covers concepts appearing in 1-2 questions per exam, often in conceptual form rather than calculation-heavy.

Damped Oscillations

Real oscillators experience friction or resistance, causing amplitude to decrease exponentially: A(t) = A₀e^(-bt/2m). The quality factor Q = ω₀m/b determines how quickly oscillations die out. NEET asks:

  • What happens to period in damped oscillations? (Increases slightly)
  • Under what conditions is damping critical, underdamped, or overdamped?
  • Which graph represents damped oscillation? (Exponentially decreasing envelope)

Forced Oscillations and Resonance

When an external periodic force drives an oscillator, the system oscillates at the driving frequency. At resonance, when driving frequency equals natural frequency (ω_drive = ω₀), amplitude becomes maximum and energy transfer is maximum. This concept appears in:

  • Bridge collapse scenarios (2011 Tacoma Narrows reference)
  • AC circuits with RLC combinations
  • "At what frequency is amplitude maximum?" questions
Exam Pattern: Resonance typically appears as 1 conceptual question or 1 graph-reading question. Focus on recognizing resonance condition rather than detailed mathematical derivations.
