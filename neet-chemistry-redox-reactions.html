Understanding Oxidation States: The Foundation

NCERT Class XI Chemistry - Chapter 8: Redox Reactions

Oxidation state assignment is the cornerstone of redox chemistry. Every NEET question on balancing redox equations requires you to correctly identify which atoms are oxidized and which are reduced. Without mastering oxidation states, you cannot balance equations systematically.

Core Rules for Assigning Oxidation States

The NEET examination strictly follows these oxidation state rules:

  • Elements in elemental form: Oxidation state = 0 (e.g., O₂, N₂, Fe, S₈)
  • Monatomic ions: Oxidation state = charge on ion (e.g., Na⁺ = +1, Cl⁻ = -1)
  • Oxygen in compounds: Usually -2, except in peroxides (-1) and OF₂ (+2)
  • Hydrogen in compounds: Usually +1, except in metal hydrides (-1)
  • Alkali metals: Always +1; alkaline earth metals: always +2
  • Halogens: Usually -1, except when bonded to more electronegative elements
  • Sum rule: Sum of oxidation states in compound = charge on compound
🎯 NEET Tip: In NEET exams, questions often use transition metals (Mn, Cr, Fe) where multiple oxidation states are possible. Always verify your assigned oxidation states using the sum rule before proceeding with balancing. This single check prevents 80% of careless errors.

Identifying Redox Pairs

Once oxidation states are assigned, identify atoms whose oxidation states change:

  • Oxidized species: oxidation state increases (loses electrons)
  • Reduced species: oxidation state decreases (gains electrons)
  • Non-redox atoms: oxidation state remains unchanged (spectator ions)

NEET questions frequently ask "which element is oxidized" or "which is the reducing agent." The reducing agent is the species that gets oxidized; the oxidizing agent is the species that gets reduced.

The Half-Reaction Method: Your NEET Strategy

NCERT Class XI Chemistry - Chapter 8: Half-Reaction Method

The half-reaction method is the most systematic approach tested in NEET. It works in both acidic and basic solutions, and examiners prefer it because it reveals complete understanding.

Step-by-Step Half-Reaction Balancing

Step 1: Identify oxidation states and write half-reactions

Separate the equation into oxidation half-reaction (element loses electrons) and reduction half-reaction (element gains electrons).

Step 2: Balance atoms other than O and H

Ensure the number of atoms being oxidized/reduced matches on both sides.

Step 3: Balance oxygen using H₂O

In acidic medium, add H₂O on the side needing oxygen. In basic medium, also add OH⁻ after this step.

Step 4: Balance hydrogen using H⁺ (acidic) or OH⁻ (basic)

In acidic solution, use H⁺; in basic solution, use OH⁻ on both sides, then simplify.

Step 5: Balance charge using electrons

Add electrons to the more positive side until both sides have equal charge.

Step 6: Equalize electron transfer

Multiply both half-reactions so the electrons lost in oxidation equal electrons gained in reduction.

Step 7: Add half-reactions and cancel spectator species

Combine both halves and cancel H⁺, H₂O, or OH⁻ that appear on both sides.

Example (Acidic Medium):

MnO₄⁻ + Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + Fe³⁺

Reduction: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O
Oxidation: Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + e⁻ (×5)

Multiply oxidation by 5:
5Fe²⁺ → 5Fe³⁺ + 5e⁻

Add both:
MnO₄⁻ + 5Fe²⁺ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O

Balancing in Basic Medium: The Exam Variation

NCERT Class XI Chemistry - Chapter 8: Reactions in Basic Solutions

NEET examiners frequently test basic medium balancing because many students skip this. The method is identical to acidic medium, but with one crucial additional step.

Converting Acidic to Basic Solution

After balancing in acidic medium with H⁺:

  • Count H⁺ ions produced or consumed
  • Add equal moles of OH⁻ to both sides
  • Combine H⁺ with OH⁻ to form H₂O
  • Cancel H₂O that appears on both sides
⚠️ Common Error: Students often forget to cancel H₂O after forming it. In basic medium, your final equation should contain OH⁻ or O²⁻, not excess H⁺ or H₂O molecules that could be simplified. NEET marking is strict about this.
