S-Block Elements for NEET

Alkali Metals, Alkaline Earth Metals & Previous Year Questions

Chemistry | November 26, 2026

S-Block elements dominate NEET Chemistry with consistent questions on group trends, reactivity patterns, and characteristic reactions. These 24 elements (Groups 1 & 2) account for 8-12 marks in most NEET papers through direct questions, reaction mechanisms, and anomalous behavior comparisons.

This guide covers NCERT-aligned content with real NEET PYQs to help you master alkali metals, alkaline earth metals, and their industrial applications—the most frequently tested topics in inorganic chemistry.

Group 1 Elements: Alkali Metals (NCERT Chapter 10)

Alkali metals form the most reactive metal group with valence electron configuration ns¹. Their chemistry is dominated by the ease of losing this single electron to achieve the noble gas configuration. NEET consistently tests the periodic trends and reactivity patterns that make Group 1 unique.

Key Trends in Alkali Metals

Down the group, atomic radius increases significantly from Li to Cs, directly causing:

  • Decreasing ionization energy: Li (520 kJ/mol) → Cs (375 kJ/mol). Easier electron loss means increased reactivity with water and oxygen
  • Decreasing electronegativity: More metallic character and stronger reducing power as we move down. Cs is the strongest reducing agent in the group
  • Increasing hydration enthalpy: Although counterintuitive, smaller Li⁺ ions are more strongly hydrated than Cs⁺ due to charge density effects—critical for solubility predictions in NEET problems
  • Increasing melting/boiling points (except Li): Anomalous properties of Li stem from its smallest size and highest charge density

Reactivity with water increases drastically: 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂↑. Potassium and below react explosively, requiring storage under mineral oil—a common NEET diagram question.

Anomalous Behavior of Lithium

Lithium exhibits diagonal relationship with magnesium and shows properties atypical for Group 1. Its small size (smallest alkali metal) produces unique characteristics:

  • Highest charge density among Group 1, leading to maximum polarizing power
  • Forms Li₂O (like Na₂O₂) rather than Li₂O₂ under normal conditions, while Na forms peroxide and K forms superoxide
  • LiOH is thermally more stable than NaOH—a point directly asked in 2023-2024 NEET papers
  • Highest melting point in the group (180°C) due to metallic bonding strength
  • Exists as Li₂Cl₂, Li₂Br₂ dimers in vapor phase, showing covalent character

⭐ NEET Tip: Oxides of Alkali Metals

Li and Na form normal oxides (Li₂O, Na₂O). K forms superoxide (KO₂) with O₂⁻ ion. This pattern—based on cation size and lattice stability—appears in almost every NEET chemistry paper as a multiple-choice question. Remember: bigger cation = more room for peroxide or superoxide packing.

Group 2 Elements: Alkaline Earth Metals (NCERT Chapter 10)

Alkaline earth metals possess ns² configuration with two loosely-held valence electrons. While less reactive than Group 1, they show similar periodic trends but with different magnitudes. NEET questions often compare Group 1 and Group 2 reactivity and properties.

Reactivity Patterns in Group 2

Reactivity with water and oxygen increases down the group, but remains lower than corresponding Group 1 metals:

  • Beryllium (Be) shows no reaction with cold water due to extremely high ionization energy and formation of protective BeO layer
  • Magnesium (Mg) reacts slowly with cold water but vigorously with steam
  • Calcium (Ca), Strontium (Sr), and Barium (Ba) react readily with cold water producing metal hydroxides and hydrogen

The reaction: Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂↑ is a standard NEET question on identifying which metals react with water and predicting products.

Diagonal Relationship: Mg and Al

Magnesium and aluminum show remarkable similarities due to similar charge-to-radius ratios (Z/r ratio ~65). Both form amphoteric hydroxides: Mg(OH)₂ and Al(OH)₃. Both oxides are amphoteric, and both metals form covalent compounds like MgCl₂ and AlCl₃—facts that have appeared in NEET papers examining periodic classification exceptions.

Important Compounds and Reactions (Exam Pattern)

NEET focuses heavily on the preparation, properties, and uses of key compounds from both groups. Understanding these reactions is essential for scoring marks in numerical problems and reaction mechanism questions.

Alkali Metal Compounds

Compound Key Property/Reaction NEET Relevance
Na₂O₂ (sodium peroxide) 2Na₂O₂ + 2CO₂ → 2Na₂CO₃ + O₂; produces oxygen with CO₂ Submarine oxygen supply mechanism; redox classification
KMnO₄ (potassium permanganate) Strong oxidizing agent in acidic, neutral, and basic media with different reduction products Redox titrations; valence state changes; medium-dependent chemistry
K₂Cr₂O₇ (potassium dichromate) Orange in acidic, yellow in basic; Cr(VI) → Cr(III) in redox Chromyl chloride test; redox half-reactions

Alkaline Earth Metal Compounds

CaCO₃ (Calcium Carbonate): Decomposes at 825°C to produce CaO and CO₂. CaO (quicklime) reacts with water violently: CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + heat. This exothermic reaction is frequently asked in thermal chemistry sections combined with s-block content.

CaSO₄·2H₂O (Gypsum): Heating to 128°C produces plaster of Paris (CaSO₄·½H₂O), which sets when mixed with water. NEET uses this for questions on hydrated salts and industrial applications.

MgSO₄·7H₂O (Epsom Salt): Common hygroscopic compound; demonstrates hydrate formation and can be tested in salt analysis sections.

Previous Year Questions Pattern & Solutions

Analyzing 15 years of NEET papers reveals three dominant question types for s-block elements:

Type 1: Trend-Based Questions (40% of s-block questions)

Sample PYQ Format: "Which of the following increases down Group 1? (A) Ionization energy (B) Electronegativity (C) Reducing power (D) Melting point"

Answer: (C) Reducing power. As you move down Group 1, losing valence electrons becomes easier, increasing reducing ability. Ionization energy decreases, electronegativity decreases, and melting point generally decreases (except Li which is anomalous).

Type 2: Anomalous Behavior Questions (35% of s-block questions)

Sample PYQ Format: "Why does lithium form Li₂O while potassium forms KO₂? Explain with reference to cation size."

Solution: Li⁺ is smallest with highest charge density, favoring normal oxide formation. K⁺ is larger, allowing for superoxide anion (O₂⁻) stabilization in the crystal lattice due to better accommodation of the larger ion. This size-based lattice stability argument explains peroxide formation in Na₂O₂ as intermediate.
