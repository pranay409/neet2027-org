Biodiversity and Conservation for NEET

Key Terms, Exam Patterns & 5-Mark Questions

Published: October 11, 2026 | Updated: NEET 2027 Exam Pattern

Biodiversity and Conservation is a critical chapter in the NEET Biology curriculum, typically carrying 4-6 marks in the exam. Questions focus on understanding species richness, conservation strategies, endangered species, and India's biodiversity hotspots. This guide provides exam-focused content aligned with NCERT Class 12 Biology Chapter 15 and supplementary NEET patterns.

Understanding Biodiversity: Definitions and Exam Focus

NCERT Class 12 Biology Chapter 15 (Part 1)

Biodiversity refers to the variety of life forms at all levels of biological organization—genetic, species, and ecosystem levels. In NEET exams, questions primarily target species-level biodiversity and its measurement.

Key Definitions Every NEET Aspirant Must Know

  • Species Diversity: The number and relative abundance of different species in an area. NEET often asks about alpha, beta, and gamma diversity—don't skip these classifications.
  • Genetic Diversity: Variation in genes within a population. Questions may ask about allelic frequency and why genetic diversity is crucial for species survival.
  • Ecosystem Diversity: Variety of different ecosystems in a region. India's Western Ghats and Northeast India are frequent answer references.
  • Endemism: Species found only in a specific geographic region. Critical for India-based questions; Western Ghats, Sri Lanka, and Andaman-Nicobar Islands are biodiversity hotspots.
  • Species Richness: Total number of species in an area (not considering abundance). Often confused with species diversity in exams.

⭐ NEET Exam Tip

Hotspot Questions Pattern: NEET regularly asks "How many of India's biodiversity hotspots are located in Western Ghats?" Answer: Western Ghats and Himalayas are two major Indian hotspots. Also know that globally there are 34-36 biodiversity hotspots (varies by study year).

Common 5-Mark Question: "Distinguish between alpha, beta, and gamma diversity with examples." Practice defining each with specific ecosystem examples.

Global and Indian Biodiversity Hotspots

NCERT Class 12 Biology Chapter 15 (Part 2)

A biodiversity hotspot is a region with high species endemism and significant habitat loss. NEET exams frequently test knowledge of these regions, especially India's hotspots.

India's Biodiversity Hotspots (Exam Priority)

  • Western Ghats: Contains 4,000+ plant species, 30% endemic. Home to Asian elephants, Bengal tigers, and Nilgiri tahr. Expected in 2-3 mark questions.
  • Himalayan Region: Highest altitude biodiversity in India. Contains musk deer, snow leopard, and alpine flora. Often paired with conservation questions.
  • Northeast India (Indo-Burma): High endemism rate. Hosts one-horned rhinoceros in Assam.
  • Andaman and Nicobar Islands: Marine biodiversity hotspot. 95% of reptiles are endemic.
Typical NEET Question Pattern:
"Which of the following regions has the highest rate of plant endemism in India?
A) Thar Desert
B) Western Ghats
C) Indo-Gangetic Plain
D) Deccan Plateau"

Answer: B (Western Ghats - ~30% endemism rate)
Why others are wrong: Thar has low biodiversity; Indo-Gangetic is agricultural; Deccan has lower endemism than Western Ghats.

Conservation Strategies and Implementation

NCERT Class 12 Biology Chapter 15 (Part 3)

Conservation is divided into in-situ and ex-situ approaches. NEET exams test understanding of both methods and their application in protecting endangered species.

In-Situ Conservation (Protecting Species in Natural Habitat)

  • Protected Areas: National parks, wildlife sanctuaries, and biosphere reserves. India has 104 national parks and 551 wildlife sanctuaries as of 2026.
  • National Parks: Highest protection level. Hunting and habitat modification are strictly prohibited. Examples: Jim Corbett, Kaziranga, Gir Forest.
  • Wildlife Sanctuaries: Moderate protection. Some human activity permitted. Example: Bharatpur Bird Sanctuary.
  • Biosphere Reserves: UNESCO-designated areas with core zone, buffer zone, and transition zone. India has 18 biosphere reserves. Nanda Devi Biosphere Reserve is important exam reference.

Ex-Situ Conservation (Conservation Outside Natural Habitat)

  • Zoos and Aquariums: Maintain captive populations. NEET may ask about breeding programs for endangered species.
  • Botanical Gardens: Preserve plant species. Used for research and education.
  • Seed Banks and Genetic Repositories: Store genetic material for future restoration. Critical for climate change adaptation.
  • Captive Breeding Programs: Increasing population of endangered species (e.g., Arabian oryx, Indian rhinoceros). Often tested in case study format.

🎯 High-Frequency NEET Question

Question: "Which of the following is an example of ex-situ conservation?"
A) Creating a national park
B) Maintaining a wildlife sanctuary
C) Breeding endangered species in zoos
D) Protecting habitat corridors"

Answer: C. Ex-situ means outside natural habitat (zoo breeding). Others are in-situ examples.

Study tip: Always ask "Is the organism in its natural habitat?" If NO → ex-situ. If YES → in-situ.

Threats to Biodiversity and Endangered Species

NCERT Class 12 Biology Chapter 15 (Part 4)

Understanding threats is essential for NEET questions on conservation urgency and policy implications.

Major Threats to Biodiversity

  • Habitat Destruction: Leading cause of biodiversity loss. Deforestation reduces species richness dramatically. Example: Loss of rainforests reduces biodiversity by 50% per hectare.
  • Pollution: Chemical, light, and noise pollution degrade habitats. Pesticides particularly harm amphibians and birds.
  • Overexploitation: Unsustainable hunting and fishing. Example: Tiger populations declined from 40,000 (1900s) to ~3,000 (pre-1970s).
  • Invasive Species: Non-native species outcompete native species. Example: Water hyacinth in Indian water bodies.
  • Climate Change: Alters habitat conditions, shifts species ranges. Polar bears losing Arctic ice habitat is a classic example.

India's Endangered Species (Exam Checklist)

  • Bengal Tiger: Critically endangered; Project Tiger initiated 1973. Population increased from ~1,400 (1970s) to ~3,000+ (2023).
  • Asian Elephant: Vulnerable status; conflicts with humans over land use.
  • Indian Rhinoceros: Endangered; Kaziranga National Park is primary population center.
  • Giant Panda: (Global example, not Indian) Vulnerable; successful conservation model for NEET case studies.
  • Vultures (Asian species): Critically endangered; population crashed due to veterinary diclofenac use in livestock.
Typical Reasoning Question:
"Why did Asian vulture populations collapse drastically in India during 1990s-2000s?"
A) Habitat loss
B) Diclofenac use in livestock veterinary medicine
C) Hunting for traditional medicine
D) Climate change"

Answer: B. Vultures fed on livestock carcasses treated with diclofenac, leading to kidney failure. This is a crucial conservation lesson tested in NEET reasoning questions.

Understanding these concepts comprehensively is essential because NEET questions often combine multiple topics. A single question might ask about a hotspot's conservation status, threats faced, and policy measures—testing integrated knowledge across the chapter.
